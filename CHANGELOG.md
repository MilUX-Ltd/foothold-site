# Changelog

All notable changes to the Foothold site. Versions follow semantic versioning.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Four views of the pack, chosen from a switcher over the drawing ("The whole map", "Who buys", "How a job runs", "At a glance", and the network under "More"), held in the address as `view`. One selection, one job and one path hold across them; a view that does not hold the selected page says so and links to the whole map. Pointing at a page, by mouse or keyboard focus, lights it and its named neighbours and quietens the rest. The key and the help show only what the current view uses. The wording is a proposal awaiting approval.
- The whole map: the defence reference as a wheel around the Ministry of Defence (its family on rings by depth, programmes, frameworks, portals and events in the middle ring, everyone else by family in the outer ring, each family an arc with its name and count), the business structure as a tree (every folder named and indented, skills grouped, the jobs listed), and bands from the wheel to each folder that holds defence pages, as wide as the number of pages. Zoom and movement by wheel, drag, pinch, buttons and keys; marks and labels keep their size on screen and more names appear as it zooms in. On the home page it draws itself in once, not with reduced motion. Without script it is a picture with real text.
- Who buys (programmes, the bodies the work is for or that run it, and frameworks and portals, ordered so lines cross little), how a job runs (each job, its steps, the folders they read and write and what the pack has put there), and at a glance (one mark for every page, shaded by named connections by fill as well as tone). At phone width, and wherever a drawing has no room, who buys and how a job runs become lists.
- How a job runs takes over the job routes: choosing a job or a step, in the drawing or the Jobs panel, lights it there and on the whole map. The home page's "See it on the map" links open it.
- `src/lib/graph/view-*.ts`: pure layouts for the wheel, the tree, the bands, who buys, how a job runs and at a glance, label placement for every zoom step, text measurement from the site's own fonts, and the drawings with their allow-list, each unit tested. The build writes the drawings of the views not in the page to `/map/views/`.
- Tests: unit tests for each layout, the address and the drawings' allow-list; browser tests for the switcher by mouse and keyboard, selection across views, the "not in this view" line, a job across views, restoring each view from the address, the page without script, pointing, phone width, accessibility in every view, reduced motion, the script budget and the switching time at the real data size.

### Changed
- The address: `view` now names the view; `2d` and `3d` are read as the network view. The network canvas starts only when its view is first shown, and the page says it is ready on `#explorer`. Earlier map, home and route tests wait for that signal, read the home page's drawing and the lit job from the drawn views, and open the network view where they test the canvas itself.
- The list of every page starts folded on wide screens narrower than 1,200 pixels, so the drawing has the room.

- Jobs on the map. A "Jobs" control with the map's other controls opens a panel listing the pack's routes. Choosing one lights the route, its skills and the folders they read and write, dims the rest and fits the view to what is lit. "Next step" and "Previous step", the step list and the left and right arrow keys move through it; each step shows its skill, the pack's sentence, the folders it reads and writes and how many defence pages those folders hold, and lights the skill and its folders strongly, the defence pages softly and the job's other steps faintly, each tier differing by size and outline as well as tone. A skill that reads the whole vault lights the vault mark and says "the whole vault". When a step reads or writes the folder the example business is filed in, the map names it and the panel says so in one line. Each change is announced. `route` and `step` are held in the address; "Close the job", closing the panel or asking for a path returns the ordinary map. Each job on the home page links to it with "See it on the map". The wording is a proposal awaiting approval.
- `src/lib/graph/routes.ts`: a route's steps with their reads and writes, and what a job or a step lights, unit tested. The renderer only draws the tiers it is given.
- Tests: unit tests for the route view, the lit sets, the whole vault, the example business and the address; browser tests for the Jobs panel by mouse and by keyboard, every route stepped to its end, restoring from the address, the home page links, closing a job, accessibility with a job and a step open at desktop and phone width, target sizes and reduced motion.
- The home page is now the map: it fills the window under the top bar with the headline on the left over a fade to paper, and the drawing is fitted into the space the headline leaves. "Explore the map", or choosing anything on the map, puts the headline away and moves into the exploring state, where the list of every page, the selected page, the path finder, the key and the help are panels over the map that open and close. `/map/` is the same component, opening in the exploring state. At phone width the headline comes first, the map follows at a fixed share of the screen, and the panels follow as ordinary sections. Without script, the headline, the facts and the list of every page still read, and the canvas says the drawing needs script.
- The drawing: fitted to the bounds of the nodes inside the free part of the window, and fitted again when the window or a panel changes, unless the visitor has moved it; "mentions" and "filed in" lines drawn only for the page under the pointer and the selected page, with a control in the key to show them all; labels placed largest node first and left out where they would collide, inside the free area, with a paper halo; quieter region tints, titles "Defence reference" and "Your business structure", and the two regions pulled closer together.
- `src/lib/graph/fit.ts` (fitting to a rectangle, and the free area left by panels), `labels.ts` (label placement) and `edge-filter.ts` (which edges are drawn), each unit tested.
- Tests: unit tests for fitting, label placement and the edge filter; browser tests for the landing and exploring states, the headline not covering the drawing, focus on entering the map, the key's control, phone width, accessibility with each panel open, the page without script and reduced motion.
- The map page, `/map/`: the pack drawn as one map with two regions, the defence reference and the business structure, joined by the edges that cross between them. Pan, zoom, hover and select on a canvas; layers told apart by shape and edge types by dash pattern and weight, not colour alone; a flat view by default and a 3D view that tilts the two layers apart (no automatic rotation). Beside it, in ordinary HTML built at build time: a searchable list of every node grouped by kind, a panel that lists the selected node's connections by type and direction, controls to find the shortest path between two nodes with the steps listed in text, and a live region that announces selection and path results. The map and the list drive each other; everything works by keyboard; nothing animates when reduced motion is asked for. The selected node, the path ends and the view mode are held in the address.
- `src/lib/graph/`: pure modules for the graph model, the build-time layout (seeded, sorted, deterministic), route-finding, connection grouping, the address state, the viewer's data file and the drawing styles, each unit tested. The layout is settled at build time and written to `/map/graph.json`, which the viewer fetches from the site itself.
- A link to the map in the site navigation and one line on the home page pointing to it.
- Tests: unit tests for the model, layout (including a real-size timing run), route-finding, connections, address state and data file; browser tests for the map page covering the list, keyboard selection, restoring from the address, paths, accessibility in three states, reduced motion, requests and the security policy, the redraw time at the real data size, the script budget and the earlier product's name.

### Changed
- The fixture gains a Context folder, a made-up example business filed in it, and a "reads" edge from bid-response to Context, so a route step touches the example business's folder. The vault keeps no edges. The fixture counts in the model, map-function and home-page tests change to match.
- The map's controls gain "Jobs" as the first of the panel controls.
- The home page's wording follows the proposal awaiting approval: the kicker, headline, purpose sentence, fact labels and the two actions. The early-build note is removed. The home page now loads the map's bundled script.
- The map's controls sit over the map in a corner; the path finder opens from them, which is why one map-page test now presses "Find a path" first and matches the "To" field exactly.
- The content security policy now allows script and data requests from the site itself (`script-src 'self'; connect-src 'self'`) and still nothing inline and no other host.
- The bundle-size check now allows bundled module scripts from `/_astro/`, fails on any inline script, script from anywhere else, `javascript:` link or inline handler, and holds all script to a 300 KB compressed budget.
- The fixture gains the pack's vault node, which has no edges, so the tests can ask for a path that does not exist.
- The walking skeleton, at version 0.1.0 (not yet released or deployed): one page built from the real map data, with the whole baseline in place so every later card follows the same path.
- `scripts/fetch-data.mjs` fetches `data/foothold-map.json` from the public pack repository (`FOOTHOLD_DATA_REF`, default `main`), or reads the committed fixture (`FOOTHOLD_DATA_SOURCE=fixture`), checks it against the schema in `src/lib/map-schema.mjs` and writes the checked copy to `.foothold/`. It refuses a malformed file, a contact node, a node with a field that can name a person, an edge or route step that points at a missing node, and a `schema_version` whose major version is not 1.
- `src/lib/map.ts`: counts by kind, the fact strip, routes with each step resolved to its skill name, and a node's edges grouped by type.
- The home page and a 404 page: the Foothold mark and name, the proposition, a fact strip drawn from the data, the pack's routes with their ordered steps, and a plain note that this is an early build. No client-side JavaScript.
- Security headers (`public/_headers`) with no inline script or style and no third-party host; the dev and preview servers send the same headers.
- Checks: bundle size (fails on any script), public-terms guard (decision-log ids, card references, vault paths, home paths, em-dashes), and a post-deploy header check that is not wired to run yet.
- Unit tests (schema, map functions, headers, guards) and Playwright tests with axe at WCAG 2.2 AA, including a 375px width check.
- The pipeline (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) with every deploy step off until `DEPLOY_ENABLED` is set; Dependabot; code owners; the agent guard in `.claude/`.
- Repository documents: README, build context (`CLAUDE.md`), domain language and data contract (`CONTEXT.md`), lessons, six decision records and a threat model.
